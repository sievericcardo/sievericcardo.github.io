Socio-technical systems can benefit from self-adaptive digital twins that combine runtime models, simulation, formal reasoning, and operational data to support continuous monitoring, analysis, and runtime decision making. This paper presents the BedreFlyt _Artifact_, a reusable software system implementing a self-adaptive digital twin for hospital resource allocation. The artifact shows how semantic reflection, lifecycle management, optimisation, and simulation can be integrated into an executable Digital Twin to support adaptive decision making under changing operational conditions.
The BedreFlyt _Artifact_ combines semantic reflection, lifecycle management, simulation, optimisation, and a human-in-the-loop dashboard to maintain a runtime representation of a hospital and generate bed bay allocation recommendations under changing operational conditions. The software system is organised as a modular architecture that integrates these components into an executable digital twin.
The paper describes the architecture of the artifact, its deployment and use, and shows how its capabilities can be used for hospital bed bay allocation across multiple wards. The BedreFlyt _Artifact_ serves as an executable software exemplar for exploring self-adaptive digital twins and runtime decision support in hospital resource allocation.
